# Decisions and history

What was decided and why, grouped by area, plus the finished phases. This is a record to search when you need the reason for something, not something to read before starting work. Open items are in `ROADMAP.md`; how each area works now is in its `CLAUDE.md`.

Add one line per decision, under its area, newest last: `- D Mon: what was decided, and why.`

## Tree layout
- 8 Oct: own tree layout, informed by other libraries.
- 8 Oct: the orange missing-children placeholder only shows when every known child is in view; children hidden by focus get an edge marker ("+2 children") instead.
- 9 Oct: added V11 (animated transitions between tree states) to Phase C, since it depends on how the new layout positions people.
- 9 Oct: someone with several partners sits between them, earliest partnership on the left (Albert – Margaret – George), so each set of children hangs under its own couple; this overrides "father on the left" for them only. A partner's parents are placed above them on the grandparents' row (the Murphys above Bridget), not as a separate group.
- 9 Oct: layout method: rows by generation; units (a person and their partners) ordered by walking down families, in-laws' parents inserted above them; x positions by alternating sweeps (children under parents, parents over children) with order and gaps kept by isotonic regression. Lines are computed from card positions alone, which is what lets V11 animate cards and re-route lines each frame.
- 9 Oct: sibling sets either side of a couple (V8): **strict age order wins** (user's call). Each family's children stay in birth order; when both partners' parents are in view, the spouse's brothers and sisters go beyond the other family's children on the spouse's side (so a line may cross, with a hop). Moving the married child to the edge facing their spouse was tried and set aside for now.
- 9 Oct: photo cards are wider (204px) than compact cards (156px); the layout takes the card width as a parameter.
- 9 Oct: layout rules for what the demo exposed. Three or more partners: earlier partnerships on the left (the first half), later on the right, each partner's other partners further out; partners with someone between them are joined by a raised line just above the cards, and their children's line drops through the gap just inside the outer partner (never through a card). Two families intermarrying more than once: only one couple can sit where the families meet, so the other spouse joins their partner within that family's run, and their own parents' line reaches over, hopping where it crosses. Lines never cross between a couple and their two sets of parents: if the parents are placed the other way round, a set that is free to move (no parents of their own in view) moves; if neither can (e.g. cousins whose parents are brother and sister, in age order), the couple swaps, wife on the left. The demo's people link to their sources.
- 9 Oct: V13/V14 prototyped (branch `wide-trees`), as a Layout menu in the tree's zoom bar (per-browser, like Photos): cards with the photo beside the name / above it (120 × 132) / no photos, and generations top to bottom / left to right. A left-to-right tree is the same layout worked out on its side (card width and height swapped) and flipped across the diagonal (`layout/tree.ts`), so every rule and check holds in both directions; hops bulge sideways on vertical lines. Opening view of the demo on a 1400 × 700 screen: wide cards 32%, portrait 51%, left to right 45% (tall rather than wide), both together 22%.
- 9 Oct: V13/V14 settled. Layout is Vertical (generations top to bottom; narrow 112px cards, the photo across the top less the padding, the name wrapped under it; 112 × 170 with photos, 112 × 72 without) or Horizontal (left to right; the wide cards, photo on the left). The Photos switch only shows or hides photos (and re-fits the view). The zoom bar is in groups: − % +, Fit (an icon), Photos (a switch), Layout (Vertical/Horizontal with a chevron). Research-stage dots sit at the cards' bottom right; "in progress" is a firmer half-tone.
- 9 Oct: fewer crossings (from a real tree: half-brothers pushed to the far side by in-laws). Force layout was considered and rejected (rules become soft, small edits reshuffle rows, local minima); instead, standard crossing reduction on top of the rules: rows split into blocks that must stay whole, reordered by their relatives' positions in sweeps down and up, plus neighbouring-block swaps that let the rest settle; a change is kept only if it doesn't leave a family away from its children or a couple's siblings on both sides, and fewer lines cross (a person's own families crossing counts double). "Uncross" now swaps the couple instead of moving the parents when that's better. On the real tree: 7 crossing lines → 3, 5 hops → 2, every rule kept; on the demo 95 → 81. Bounded work, so the same layout on every device; the demo's full tree takes about 60 ms. A child's line that would run along another family's line (and hop on top of it) is moved along their card; a check catches lines running together.
- 10 Oct: brothers and sisters whose parents aren't in view are kept side by side. Found on a real tree: a man (no partner or children in view) was stranded far along his row because his only relative in view, his brother, had married in and was placed later as an in-law. Nothing above or below pulled them together, so he stayed where he was first put. Now such a person is moved beside their siblings (in age order) once those are placed, the sideways sweep keeps them packed against each other, and the crossing step treats them as one family's block.
- 14 Oct: fewer tangles outrank conventions (owner's call, reversing 9 Oct's "strict age order wins"). Age order among brothers and sisters, father on the left and earlier partnerships on the left are now preferences, below fewest crossings and short lines; brothers and sisters still always stay together in one run. Parents may sit off-centre over their children: "parents above their children" is no longer a checked rule, and "no hop on the way to the nearest child" was tried as one and dropped (the demo's Erasmus Darwin can't meet it; the old layout didn't either).
- 14 Oct: rows are ordered first and positioned after (found on the owner's trees: in-law families, inserted one at a time by where their relatives were, landed inside other families' runs of children, and the old tidy-up couldn't see it or move a family together with its ancestors). The old construction is kept as a starting order; its blocks come from who is whose child, so split runs join up again; ancestors are ordered from the widest rows upward; whole sweeps keep the best order seen, then small changes (swaps, mirroring, turning a couple round with every other row re-sorted from it). On the owner's largest tree: 21 crossing lines → 7, 21 hops → 6, half the width, about 150 ms (was 200); the demo is faster too (about 45 ms). Positions: brothers and sisters are packed together (one pulled away by their own family no longer spreads the others to the opposite side), and children with no children of their own go back under their parents at the end.
- 14 Oct: lines that share the gap between two rows are stacked in the order with fewest hops (a line higher up hops over the others' lines down from their parents, one lower down over their lines down to their children), not first come, first served.
- 14 Oct: "more expected" brothers and sisters follow the focus width like the rest: hidden in a direct-line view (the families the focus person and their ancestors were born into, `focusSet().noPlaceholders`), shown with siblings or all relatives. A placeholder for the focus person's or a descendant's missing children still shows, since that view shows children.

## Data, dates, places and exports
- 8 Oct: life events removed from UI (model keeps birth/death as events); notes, free-text sources, links and tags kept; "Known as" added.
- 9 Oct: place search is explicit ("Search OpenStreetMap for …"), never as you type: Nominatim's usage policy forbids client-side autocomplete. Places already in the tree are still suggested as you type.
- 9 Oct: E3/E4 done: the app starts blank, with "Load the demo family" on the empty tree and in the Data dialog. The demo is the Darwin–Wedgwood family (95 people, 1731–1989, nobody living): Erasmus Darwin's three partners, Francis Darwin's three marriages, step-families, two cousin marriages and a third Darwin–Wedgwood link through Charles Langton, genuine gaps (Elizabeth Pole's unnamed children, the Galtons' seven-or-nine children) and source conflicts (Caroline Darwin's birth year, Frances Cornford's death date). Facts from Wikipedia, the Darwin Correspondence Project and thepeerage.com; the Smith sample stays as the test fixture.
- 9 Oct: E1/E2 done. Data dialog → "For other family tree apps": Export GEDCOM (.ged, 5.5.1, UTF-8; `export/gedcom.ts`) and Export ZIP, with photos (`export/zip.ts`: family-tree.json, family-tree.ged with OBJE/FILE links, photos/ named after each person, README.txt). Certainty is kept as `_STATUS` tags under the fact (names, dates, places, child links) plus a readable "Certainty" note; research stage, to-dos, tags and links go in a "Research" note; Wikidata as REFN/TYPE; expected children as NCHI; men as HUSB, women as WIFE. The ZIP opens again through "Open backup file…" and counts as a backup; GEDCOM alone doesn't. GEDCOM import is still to do.

- 11 Oct: names (schema 0.3): the first name is the birth name, as in GEDCOM, Gramps and FamilySearch; `preferred` now means "show this name on the tree" (GEDCOM X's sense), so a married name can be shown without becoming the name that's edited first; "Known as" is for nicknames and titles. The panel shows "born …" under a different shown name and folds other names into one line. Breaking changes are converted once by a script (`scripts/migrate-0.3.mjs`), not by the app, since only the family edition holds real data.
- 12 Oct: sources (schema 0.3): free-text sources, links and Wikidata ids become one shared `Source` (type, title, web address, year), cited by people with an optional note of what it tells us, like GEDCOM's `SOUR`/`PAGE`, but only "sources" are shown: the note is part of the source's line. Shared like places, so one marriage certificate serves the couple and both sets of parents; sources link to people only (a family's scope is too unclear). Free-text sources join the notes. Anything unused (sources, places) is removed. The married-name prompts and a changeable main-name type were dropped: the main name holds whatever name is known.
- 12 Oct: dates (schema 0.3): the Confirmed / Likely / Guess switch under each date is gone; EDTF already says how sure a date is, so you type it: "maybe 1858" or "1858?" (pencil), "c.1858" (about, ink: rough but can be well sourced), "1800 or 1801" (EDTF's one-of set: sources disagree, pencil). EDTF has one level of doubt, so "likely" dates became plain. A help page with examples (`static/dates.html`) is linked as "Uncertain dates?".
- 12 Oct: release 0.3 (names, sources, dates, marriage places and place certainty) merged and the family edition's data converted once: Download backup → `node scripts/migrate-0.3.mjs` (and its "to check by hand" list) → `npm run validate` → push and wait for the deploy → Data → "Replace the shared tree with a backup…" → everyone reloads → validate a fresh backup. The app carries no code for it; repeat this for future breaking changes.
- 12 Oct: places: where a couple married is `relationship.place` (no conversion needed), shown on the map ("Married" or "Partners") and exported. Every place (birth, death, marriage) has a Confirmed / Likely / Guess switch under it, and a guessed place is in pencil.
- 14 Oct: exports can take only the current view: "Only export data for current filtered view" in the Data dialog (off by default; shown only when the view shows some people but not everyone) applies to the backup, GEDCOM and ZIP. `subsetDataset` (`model/subset.ts`) keeps those people, their families cut down to them, their events, and the places, sources and photos those use; saved views are left out. The file name gets the view's name, and a partial backup doesn't count towards the backup reminder. The dialog's buttons are grouped as "Backup and import data" and "Export for other apps", side by side, wrapping on phones.

- 8 Oct: search only centres. Someone hidden by the current focus gets their person panel with "Show in full tree" / "Focus on them"; the view doesn't change by itself. Focus is started from the panel's Focus button (or F).
- 8 Oct: focus is kept in the page address, so Back undoes a focus change and a focus view can be bookmarked.
- 8 Oct: a focus with up 0 and down 0 still shows the person's partners. (Superseded 9 Oct: see below.)
- 8 Oct: when a relative added from the person panel falls outside the focus, the notice offers "Expand view" (the smallest change to up/down/width that shows them) or "Focus on them". It falls back to "Show in full tree" only when no widening can include them (e.g. a partner's parents). The notice clears when the panel closes or the focus changes.
- 9 Oct: saved views are chips under the header (replacing the auto-detected branch chips), with "Everyone" first and "+ New view". One rule per view: a focus (root + up/down/width; "all generations" = no limit stored), everyone with a tag, or a hand-picked list. - 9 Oct: V2 "reset" leaves an empty canvas with nobody selected (`#start` in the address) and a prompt to search, open a saved view or show everyone. Choosing someone in search from the empty canvas focuses on them (elsewhere search only centres).
- 9 Oct: animation: people entering fade in; people leaving go at once (fading them out while others moved looked odd).
- 9 Oct: brothers and sisters recorded with no parents at all show with the "+ Siblings" (or "All relatives") setting, whatever the generations, except when collapsed.
- 9 Oct: map (V12): a Map tab with a MapLibre globe and OpenFreeMap tiles (positron, or dark in dark mode); a dot per place sized by its births and deaths (count inside), filtered by the current focus or view; clicking a dot lists who was born or died there; places written by hand are listed as "not shown" with a pointer to "Find on the map". MapLibre loads only when the tab opens.

- 9 Oct: timeline tooltips (styled, instant, also on keyboard focus): marriage circles show the kind of relationship, the partner, the dates with how sure they are, and how it ended. Tooltips are for events only: each name has its years beside it in grey, as on the tree cards. The legend explains the research-progress dot (empty sketch, half-tone in progress, filled researched, grey not set).

No "+ New person", no clickable missing-children placeholders, and views can be opened, focused and filtered but not saved, renamed or deleted (they're shared data). The person panel can now confirm a parent link ("This parent link is") and a parent added as a guess.

- 14 Oct: the face for people whose sex isn't known is now Material's face_2 (was face).
- 14 Oct: sources in the person panel are cards (chosen from mockups over a pencil-to-edit list and an edit sheet): tapping one opens it in place, a chevron top right; the web link is a separate "wikitree.com ↗" pill; "Remove this source" sits inside the open card (it only takes the source off this person unless no one else cites it). New sources open with their name selected. Page titles can't be read from the browser (sites block it, WikiTree's API too), so names come from the link where it says who the page is about: "WikiTree: Darwin-11", "Find a Grave: Charles Robert Darwin". - 14 Oct: timeline events: every dated marriage or partnership (ring), divorce, separation or annulment (diamond; an end by death is just the end of the bar) and child's birth (upward triangle, on both parents' rows) is drawn on the bar, from `timelineEvents` in `layout/timeline.ts`. All three are hollow, so they show on the ink bar; pencil grey when the date isn't sure. Hovering or focusing one draws a thin vertical line to the partner's or child's bar (dashed when the relationship or parent link is likely, a guess or adoptive); its tooltip says when the other person isn't on the timeline. A legend of the three symbols sits under the intro text, styled like the header legend and shown on phones too. A bar always covers all of these events: with no death date it runs to the last one, with no birth date it starts at the first one (or 20 years before death, if that's earlier), then fades. Anyone with at least one date is on the timeline: someone with only event dates gets a bar spanning them, faded at both ends (presumed living only if their first event was within 95 years).
- 14 Oct: timeline navigation works like the tree's: the mouse wheel (⌘ + wheel in Safari) or a pinch zooms the time axis only, at the pointer (1.5–80 px a year; the year marks thin out or go down to single years, `timeTicks`); rows keep their height. Dragging (primary or middle button, or one finger) pans both ways; a trackpad's two-finger scroll is the browser's own. The bars scroll in their own box under the intro and legend, so both scroll bars stay on desktop.
- 14 Oct: timeline rows are tighter (40px, was 46) with names 2px nearer their bars; a click on a bar (or just around it) opens the person, like their name.
- 14 Oct: map groups and lines. Places within 50 screen pixels are grouped per whole zoom level (`clusterPlaces`, our own rather than MapLibre's, which can only add up numbers and so would count someone at two of the places twice). Each level is built from the one above it, so groups only split as you zoom in and tight knots need another click. A group has a soft ring; its number (and every dot's) is people, each counted once. A click on a group zooms to where it splits; only a single place opens its list. An open place has lines to where the people born there died, and those who died there were born (`placeLinks`; not people born and died there, nor marriages): one line per other place, wider for more people, with a dark tooltip on hover listing each person's birth and death place and date. Lines follow the groups at the current zoom (`linksAtLevel`): lines to places in one group are one line to its middle with all their people, they start from the middle of the open place's own group, and lines within that group are left out.
- 14 Oct: removing family links. The parents get a box in the person panel like each partnership ("Parents: not recorded" stays plain text), and both kinds of box end with "Remove this relationship", one click, no confirmation. For parents it takes the person away from both (so "Not recorded" left the "Child of" list); for a partner with no children the couple goes, and with children both stay their parents and only the partnership's details are cleared (`removePartnership`), the box then saying "These people share children but have no recorded relationship".
- 14 Oct: the header by width, after a mockup of every band (M16): 1200 and up one row as before; 900–1199 one row with "To-do" (and "+" alone to 1099), only the search box shrinking, so the buttons never drop a row unless the backup reminder needs it; 480–899 two fixed rows (name and buttons; tabs and a real search box), with the Focus chip and sheet as on phones; under 480 the phone layout. The legend moved from under the views onto the tree: a card bottom left when the tree is 1100px or wider, else a "Legend" button in the same corner; "uncertain or adoptive link" and "divorced or separated".
- 14 Oct: person panel tidying: one 7px gap between everything (the name section is now a block like the others, without the rule above it); "Progress" beside the research switches; ticked checkboxes in ink like a selected switch (`accent-color`).
- 15 Oct: someone marked as researched drops their own to-dos from the Research to-do list (they stay on the person and come back if the stage changes); their other gaps (missing dates, guesses) still show.

## Shared family edition and deploying
- 9 Oct: the browser-only edition must be deployable as static assets (SvelteKit static adapter) to a host such as GitHub Pages: base path from the build environment, 404.html fallback, .nojekyll, published by a GitHub Actions workflow.
- 9 Oct: E5/E6. Deploys to GitHub Pages from `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` on each push to `main` (tests and type check first); `BASE_PATH` sets the sub-path, the fallback is `404.html`, `static/.nojekyll` keeps `_app/`. The browser is asked to keep the data (`navigator.storage.persist()`) once there's a tree; the Data dialog shows the last backup and whether the browser agreed; a "Back up your tree" reminder appears in the header when changes have gone three days without a backup (× snoozes it three days). Loading a backup or the demo doesn't count as a change. Privacy wording: the tree isn't uploaded anywhere; only place searches (to OpenStreetMap), map tiles and fonts are fetched from other servers.
- 9 Oct: deployed: https://bothness.github.io/family-tree/ (public repo, Pages source "GitHub Actions"; every push to `main` redeploys).
- 9 Oct: shared data: Cloudflare (Pages + a Worker + D1 + R2) rather than a GitHub repo (every editor would need a GitHub account, and sign-in still needs a server) or Supabase (free projects pause after a week idle). One codebase, two builds (public browser-only, private family edition). Sign-in with Google, written ourselves rather than Cloudflare Access, to avoid per-user pricing past 50 people; invite list in D1. Own passwords and passkeys were considered; Google is enough for this family.
- 9 Oct: F1 done: the three-way merge and its tests, including 25 seeded random runs of real edits on two devices (all valid data with every reference in place, whichever side each conflict takes).
- 9 Oct: no clash screens: clashes are rare and rarely destructive, so they're settled automatically (newer edit wins; an edited person is never lost to someone else's delete).
- 9 Oct: F2 done (`sync/engine.ts`, `sync/http.ts`, `sync/start.ts`, `sync/patch.ts`). `SYNC=cloudflare` sets `__SYNC__`; the sync code is loaded only then (the public build doesn't contain it). The engine keeps the last synced version on the device (`getExtra`/`putExtra` on the store), stamps changes before sending, merges and retries when someone saved first, checks every 30 seconds and when the tab comes back or the device goes online, and works offline. Incoming changes are merged with what's on screen and patched into the live data in place. A device syncing for the first time takes the shared tree and keeps what it had aside (`before-sync`). Photos upload when added (retried) and are fetched when missing. Header shows a small status (Saved / Saving / Offline / Not signed in / No access). API: GET /api/me, GET/PUT /api/tree, GET/PUT /api/media/:id.
- 9 Oct: F3 done. `functions/api/[[path]].ts` (the Pages Function) → `server/api.ts` (routes, roles, checks) → `server/repo.ts` (D1 + R2). Each tree version's data is a file in R2 under its own key (a big tree with photo thumbnails can pass D1's 2 MB row limit); D1's `tree_versions` is the list and the lock (a version number can only be inserted once, so of two racing saves one wins and the other gets the newest back). History: the last 200 versions, then one a day. Photos in R2 (`media/<id>`, images up to 10 MB, cached as immutable). Viewers read, owners and editors save. Until F4, a local-only stand-in user (`DEV_USER` in `.dev.vars`, honoured only on localhost). `npm run dev:family` builds with SYNC=cloudflare and runs it all locally on wrangler's emulator; tested end to end in a browser (load, edit, another device's change arriving, photos).
- 9 Oct: F4 done. `server/google.ts` checks the Google ID token (RS256 signature against Google's published keys, cached as Google says; issuer, audience = our client id, expiry, verified email). `server/session.ts`: a random 32-byte token in an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie (`__Host-` and Secure on the real site), only its SHA-256 stored in D1 (`sessions`, 30 days); writes must be JSON (or a photo), so other sites can't act for you. `OWNER_EMAIL` lets the first owner in; owners manage the invite list in the app (People with access: invite by Google email, roles Owner / Can edit / Can view, remove; the tree keeps at least one owner). The invite list is checked on every request; removing someone deletes their sessions. App: a sign-in screen over everything while signed out (Google's button, loaded only there; the client id comes from GET /api/config, so it can change without a rebuild); not-invited people see which address they used. The sync status opens an account menu (who you are, People with access, Sign out). Signing out removes the tree, photos and settings from the device. Viewers' changes stay on their device (never sent). In the family edition the demo isn't offered, replacing the whole tree (a backup, pasted data, Start empty) is for owners, and there's no backup reminder.
- 9 Oct: F5. The family edition is live at https://mbfamilytree.pages.dev (Cloudflare Pages project `mbfamilytree`, Direct Upload, production branch `main`; D1 `family-tree`, R2 bucket `family-tree`, both in the owner's Cloudflare account). `OWNER_EMAIL` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` are set as encrypted settings on the Pages project. The workflow's `family` job builds it (`npm run build:family`, which adds `static-family/privacy.html`), applies D1 migrations and deploys, once the `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` repository secrets exist. Privacy page: https://mbfamilytree.pages.dev/privacy (for Google's consent screen; authorised domain `mbfamilytree.pages.dev`). (`ourfamilytree.pages.dev` was taken, so Cloudflare would have added a suffix.)
- 9 Oct: icons and sharing. The app icon (a small tree in the accent green) as favicon.svg, favicon.ico (16, 32, 48), apple-touch-icon.png (180, on the paper colour: iOS fills transparency with black), icon-192/512.png and site.webmanifest, all with transparent backgrounds otherwise. The public edition has a title, description, canonical link, Open Graph and Twitter tags, and og-image.jpg (1200 × 630: Charles and Emma's family from the demo, vertical cards). They're in app.html (link previews and search engines don't run the app); the family edition's build (`scripts/family-head.mjs`) swaps them for noindex, and its robots.txt disallows everything.

### Public, browser-only edition: the plan (9 Oct; mostly built since)
Once the app is complete: a version anyone can use to build their own family tree, with all data kept in their browser (no account, no server). Possibly a fork, but better as a build setting of the same app so the two don't drift apart.

What this means for decisions made before then:
- **Storage stays behind the `DataStore` interface**, with a browser store as a first-class option, not just a fallback. Any sync with a back end (GitHub files, Cloudflare D1) is an extra layer on top, so the app works fully with no server.
- **Browser storage needs more room:** localStorage holds about 5 MB, which photos (V10) would quickly fill. Move the browser store to IndexedDB, with images kept as blobs there.
- **Export and import are the backup:** with no server, the Data dialog's JSON download and upload is how people keep and move their tree. Consider a single-file export that includes photos, and a gentle reminder to back up.
- **No server-only features:** place lookup (G6) must be able to call Nominatim or Wikidata directly from the browser (both allow it, within usage limits), not only through our own proxy.
- **Place lookup at scale:** Nominatim's public service allows moderate use only, at most one request a second in total across all of an app's users, and forbids search-as-you-type. That's fine for one family, but a public edition with many users may need another geocoding service (e.g. Photon, or a commercial one) or our own proxy with caching. Keep the lookup behind `places/` so it can be swapped.
- **Multi-editor features stay optional:** `meta` provenance and IDs already work for a single user; anything needing accounts goes behind the sync layer.
- **Privacy wording:** say plainly that data stays on this device, and that clearing browser data deletes it unless exported.
- **Deployable as static files:** the browser-only edition must build with SvelteKit's static adapter (`adapter-static`, already in use) and run from a plain static host such as **GitHub Pages**, with no server code. For GitHub Pages specifically:
  - Serve from a sub-path (`username.github.io/family-tree/`): set `kit.paths.base` from an environment variable at build time, and keep all links and asset URLs relative to it.
  - The SPA fallback must be `404.html` there (GitHub Pages serves it for unknown paths), not `200.html` as now. All app state is in the `#…` part of the address, so only the root page is ever requested.
  - Add an empty `.nojekyll` file to the build, or Pages' Jekyll step hides SvelteKit's `_app/` folder.
  - A GitHub Actions workflow can build and publish on each push to `main`.
  - Everything the app loads at runtime must work from there: the MapLibre worker (already bundled as a static asset), OpenFreeMap tiles and Nominatim (called from the browser).
